Twelve misread names is a calibration problem

A tts voice that mangles your product name is not a broken model. It is a missing calibration set. The reflex fix is a prompt appendix: always say Velexa as veh-LEX-ah. That line rides along on every request, and it still loses the argument now and then, because a model reading text aloud treats your instruction as strong advice rather than a rule.

The alternative is to stop arguing and start measuring. Take the twelve names, model numbers, and abbreviations your product says out loud, put one on each line, and have the voice read the list. That is the whole test. Two minutes of audio, and it runs on every model or stock voice you are thinking of switching to.

Say two of the twelve rows come back mangled. The targeted rewrite for those two lines is the entire fix. A rewrite swaps about forty characters of a two-hundred-character line, roughly ten extra input tokens at $3 a million, an extra three thousandths of a cent per call. The pronunciation risk on that row drops to zero, because the text the model receives is already phonetic.

The bill that does move is the retry loop nobody prices. When a take comes back wrong, someone re-renders it. The re-render itself costs the same tokens as a clean take, about eight tenths of a cent for five hundred output tokens at $15 a million. The real cost is the human minute spent noticing, resubmitting, and relistening. At $30 an hour that minute is fifty cents, sixty-six times the price of the take it flags.

So the arithmetic lands like this: twelve-line calibration list, built once in an afternoon, two rows rewritten at a surcharge of three thousandths of a cent per call. Run the two rewritten lines a thousand times a day and the rewrite surcharge totals three cents a day. One avoided re-render per day, at one minute of staff time, saves a cent and a quarter, which covers the surcharge forty times over.

This is the discipline PastAGI applies to serving-cost audits on AI systems, and the site's own TontaubeV1 review makes the same move for a char-level tts model: price the repeated failure, not the visible line item. Rewrites live in the input, where tokens are cheap and the result is deterministic. Retries live in the output, where tokens cost five times as much and the outcome still depends on the model's mood that day.
